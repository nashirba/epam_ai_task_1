# Fitch affirms Kazakhstan at BBB+ with stable outlook

Fitch Ratings affirmed Kazakhstan's Long-Term Foreign-Currency IDR at BBB+ with a stable outlook on April 25, 2026. The agency cited the country's strong external balance sheet, active sovereign wealth management framework, and prudent fiscal stance.

In its commentary Fitch noted that Kazakhstan's gross general government debt, at approximately 25% of GDP, is well below the BBB-median of 55%. The National Fund of Kazakhstan, with assets of $65.8 billion as of Q1 2026, provides a substantial fiscal buffer. External liquidity remains strong, with current-account balance returning to surplus in Q1.

Risks flagged include: high commodity-export concentration (oil and gas account for ~60% of goods exports), heightened geopolitical risk from regional conflicts, and the country's structural challenges in diversifying away from extractive industries. Fitch projects real GDP growth at 4.6% for 2026 and 4.3% for 2027.

The agency expects inflation to fall to 6.5% by end-2026 from 7.6% in April, supported by the National Bank's tight policy stance and KZT stability. Public-sector wage growth and tariff adjustments remain key inflation risks.

Yields on Kazakhstan's 2030 USD eurobond fell 8 bps to 5.18% following the affirmation. The KZT 30-year sovereign curve also tightened modestly. Local-currency bond yields were broadly unchanged, with the 10-year MEUKAM trading at 13.1%.
