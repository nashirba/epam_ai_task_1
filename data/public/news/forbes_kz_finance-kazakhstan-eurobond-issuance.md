# Kazakhstan placed $1.5bn of eurobonds at competitive spreads

The Republic of Kazakhstan returned to international debt markets on April 9, placing two tranches of eurobonds totalling $1.5 billion. A 7-year tranche of $750 million was priced at 5.35% (UST + 95 bps), and a 12-year tranche of $750 million at 5.75% (UST + 130 bps).

Demand exceeded supply by 4.2x for the 7-year and 3.8x for the 12-year tranche. Asian and European investors took 62% of the allocation; US accounts received 28%. The deal was led by Citi, JPMorgan and Halyk Finance.

Proceeds will be used for general budget needs and to refinance maturing 2027 dollar paper. The placement is the first sovereign eurobond issuance from Kazakhstan since 2024 and confirms strong investor appetite for KZ credit in the EM frontier space.

Kazakhstan's sovereign credit ratings are Baa1 (Moody's), BBB (S&P) and BBB+ (Fitch), all with stable outlook. The country's external debt-to-GDP is around 28%, among the lowest in the EM peer group.

"The execution and pricing reflect the market's confidence in Kazakhstan's macroeconomic stability — strong external balance sheet, active sovereign wealth fund, prudent fiscal policy. Investor questions focused on the trajectory of NBK base rate and the planned reforms in the energy sector," said Halyk Finance head of fixed income.

Domestic banks priced their dollar bond curves tighter following the sovereign deal. Halyk Bank, BCC and Forte are reportedly preparing senior unsecured eurobond issues for the second half of 2026.
