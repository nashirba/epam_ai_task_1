# Tenge among strongest CIS currencies in Q1

The Kazakhstani tenge gained 0.7% against the US dollar in Q1 2026, finishing the quarter at 487.20 per USD. Among CIS currencies, only the Armenian dram (+1.2%) outperformed the tenge over the same period; the Russian ruble depreciated 4.8%, the Azerbaijani manat was flat, and the Uzbekistani som lost 2.1%.

Strength has been supported by elevated oil prices (Brent averaging $84/bbl in Q1), a tight monetary policy stance, and steady inflows from Tengiz and Kashagan oil exports. Real-money flows from KZ-resident exporters into KZT have been consistent throughout the quarter.

The National Bank conducted minimal FX interventions during the quarter; net intervention volume was reported at -$140 million (selling USD), well below the average of recent quarters. The market has been able to absorb seasonal demand without significant central bank involvement.

Halyk Finance forecasts the USD/KZT pair to trade in a 478–495 range over the next six months, with risks tilted toward further KZT strength if oil prices stay above $80/bbl. The April 18 base-rate cut to 15.5% has had a muted effect — the market had largely priced in the move.

Hedge funds remained underweight KZT exposure heading into Q1 due to historical FX volatility concerns. Several large EM funds reportedly added KZT positions in March, citing the country's external surplus and stable policy framework. The carry-to-volatility ratio for KZT remains attractive vs. EM-Europe peers.
