# Deposit insurance limit raised to 25 million tenge

The Kazakhstan Deposit Insurance Fund (KDIF) raised the maximum guaranteed deposit amount from 20 million to 25 million tenge per depositor per bank, effective April 1, 2026. The change was approved by the National Bank board and represents the first increase since 2022.

The new limit applies to deposits in tenge; for foreign-currency deposits the guarantee remains at the equivalent of 5 million tenge. Multi-currency or savings products that combine FX and KZT components are covered up to 25 million tenge in aggregate, with the FX portion subject to its own sub-limit.

The KDIF reserve fund stood at 745 billion tenge at end-Q1 2026, equivalent to 3.6% of total insured deposits. The agency's stress-testing framework continues to assume coverage of any single mid-sized bank failure plus secondary contagion effects.

Industry reaction has been broadly positive. "An increased limit reduces depositor concentration risk for the largest institutions and helps maintain depositor confidence as nominal deposit balances grow with inflation. It also nudges high-net-worth households to spread balances across more banks," commented a Halyk Finance economist.

The change also brings Kazakhstan closer to the EU deposit-insurance benchmark of 100,000 EUR (~52 million tenge) and ahead of regional peers. Russia's deposit-insurance limit is RUB 1.4 million (~7.5 million tenge), Uzbekistan's covers 100% of deposits in any amount but only at state-owned banks.
