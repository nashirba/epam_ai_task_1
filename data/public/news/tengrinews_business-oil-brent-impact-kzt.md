# Oil at $88: what it means for the tenge

Brent crude breached $88 per barrel on April 21, the highest level since November 2024. For Kazakhstan — where oil and oil products account for roughly 60% of total goods exports — every $5 sustained move in Brent translates into approximately $2 billion in additional annual export revenue.

The historical correlation between Brent and USD/KZT is around -0.45 over the past five years; tenge tends to strengthen as oil prices rise, though the relationship is noisy and often lagged. Since the start of 2026, Brent has gained 7% while KZT has gained 0.7% vs USD — a smaller pass-through than in earlier cycles, partly due to the National Fund's stabilisation flows and lower volatility in the FX market.

National Fund of Kazakhstan reserves grew by approximately $1.8 billion in Q1 2026 to $65.8 billion, supported by stronger oil revenues. Government transfers from the National Fund to the budget for 2026 are budgeted at 4.0 trillion tenge, or about $8.2 billion, a level slightly below 2025.

Equity-market beneficiaries of higher oil prices include KazMunayGas (KMG), KAZ Minerals (KAZ) — though copper-leveraged — and the broader KASE Index through dividend expectations. Banks indirectly benefit via stronger corporate-loan demand and lower credit costs in the energy supply chain.

A risk to monitor: the OPEC+ meeting in early June, where Kazakhstan's quota for the second half of 2026 will be discussed. Kazakhstan has historically struggled to meet quota commitments due to project ramp-up at Tengiz and Kashagan.
