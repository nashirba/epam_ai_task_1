# Kazakhstan pension fund increases foreign-asset allocation

The Unified Accumulative Pension Fund (ENPF) of Kazakhstan increased the share of foreign assets in its portfolio to 31.4% as of April 1, 2026, up from 27.8% at the start of the year. Total ENPF assets reached 22.4 trillion tenge.

The allocation shift reflects rising new-flow capacity from contributions plus reinvestment of maturing tenge-denominated government bonds into a mix of US Treasuries, US investment-grade corporates, and global equity index funds. ENPF's investment guidelines, set by the National Bank as the asset manager, were widened in late 2025 to allow up to 40% in foreign assets vs. the previous 35% cap.

Tenge government bonds (MEKKAM and MEUKAM) still represent the largest single category at 38% of the portfolio. KASE-listed equities account for 4.2%, foreign equity ETFs 11.6%, foreign sovereign bonds 9.8%, foreign corporate bonds 9.2%, and cash equivalents 5.8%.

The fund's nominal annualised return over the trailing 12 months was 13.8%, while real return after inflation was approximately 5.3%. Performance has been driven by tenge fixed-income coupons and gains in the US equity component (S&P 500 +9.4% over the same period).

"Diversification into hard-currency assets is a structural priority. Even if the local market continues to deliver attractive nominal yields, a long-horizon retirement portfolio benefits from currency and asset-class breadth," said an ENPF spokesperson. The fund's beneficiaries received their annual statements in late April; aggregate notional return per individual account was reported at 14.1%.
